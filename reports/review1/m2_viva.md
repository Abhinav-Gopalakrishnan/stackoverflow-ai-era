# Member 2: viva questions for cleaning and sampling (M2-VIVA)

Five likely examiner questions, with short model answers. Numbers come from `notebooks/02_cleaning.ipynb` and `notebooks/02b_sampling_robustness.ipynb`. For Member 3's shared list.

| # | Likely question | Model answer |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Is an accepted answer within 7 days the same as "resolved"? Why is a question with no accepted answer a 0 and not missing or dropped? | No. Acceptance is a strict proxy: it depends on the asker returning to accept. So we also report any answer within 7 days (75% of questions) and track acceptance among answered questions over time (54%). A question with no accepted answer is a real outcome (unresolved), so it stays in the data as target 0 (171,932 questions); dropping them would leave only the resolved ones and make the model meaningless. |
| 2 | Deleted askers have no account date. How did you fill it without leaking future information? | 6,319 askers (2.1%) are imputed with the median account age of questions posted before 2025 (543 days), i.e. the training period only, and a `missing_account_age` flag tells the model which rows were imputed. Account age is also clipped at 0 for the 92 accounts that appear newer than their question. |
| 3 | Your data is sampled before 2025. How do you know trends are not distorted? | Each tag-month is one weekday and one weekend day, and each question carries a weight (days of that type in the month divided by days sampled). We checked three ways: yearly volume matches Member 1's complete counts within about 2%; weighted and unweighted resolved rates differ by under 1 point per year; and a second random seed on `sql` and `excel` gave the same overall class balance within 0.3 to 0.7 points. Single months are noisier (mean error 7%), so volume charts use the complete counts and low-sample months (under 30 questions) are greyed out. |
| 4 | A question tagged with two of your tags appears in both files. How did you avoid counting it twice? | We merged on `question_id`: 7,147 duplicates became one row each, keeping the list of tags it was collected under. When the two copies had different sampling weights (559 cases) we kept the smaller weight, the more conservative estimate of how many real questions the row stands for. Tag-level tables count the question once in each of its tags. |
| 5 | You scraped Stack Overflow. What about privacy and licence, and did cleaning remove anything based on the outcome? | Content is CC BY-SA 4.0; outputs show titles with links and a licence notice and raw text is not published. User IDs are hashed with SHA-256 and a secret salt immediately after fetching, and only the hash is stored. Cleaning never looks at the targets: it removed only 6 mostly non-English posts, capped 303 very long bodies and dropped nothing for label maturity (the newest question is 33 days old, above the 30-day rule). |
